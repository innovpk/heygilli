You pick the channels. Every new upload is screened before it can appear. And while your child watches, Gilli asks them about it — then tells you what they understood.

Hi, I'm Gilli.

What it does

Four things, and your child never signs in to any of them.

You choose the channels

Nothing reaches your child from a channel you haven't approved. No open-ended search unless you turn it on.

Every upload is screened

New videos from approved channels are checked automatically, against the rules and limits you set, before they appear.

Gilli watches along

A spoken buddy asks a question or two during and after a video, pitched to your child's age and language.

You get the digest

What they watched, what they understood, what they found hard — shown only to you, never to your child.

Why it asks for your YouTube subscriptions

Neither YouTube nor YouTube Kids exposes a way to read a child's profile. So HeyGilli reads your account's subscriptions — read-only, it never posts, uploads, or changes anything — to build the list of channels you already trust, which you then approve one by one.

You can skip that entirely: bring channels across from a Google Takeout export, or just add them yourself.

What your child never does

Sign in. Enter their name. Get recommended anything you didn't approve. Reach a video from a channel you haven't allowed.

Watch history is off by default and never stored video by video. Answers spoken to Gilli are recognised on the device — the audio itself is never uploaded. The privacy policy says exactly what is kept, and what isn't.

Try it with your own child's channels

Set up a household in a minute. Google sign-in brings your subscriptions across, or start without it and add channels yourself.
